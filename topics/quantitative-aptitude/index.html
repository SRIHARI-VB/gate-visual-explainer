Topic and objective

TOPIC: Quantitative Aptitude — Section 2 of the General Aptitude syllabus, which is common to every GATE paper including CS. Data interpretation; numerical computation and estimation (ratios, percentages, powers, exponents and logarithms, permutations and combinations, series); mensuration and geometry; elementary statistics and probability.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE: Convert a ratio into money by multiplying each share by its denomination, and express a part as a percentage of the one base the question names. Read a share from a chart, multiply by the stated total and divide by a rate from a table, checking that the units cancel. Use the two logarithm rules to turn a logarithmic condition into ordinary algebra, and square it without dropping the middle term. Decide which edge of a rectangle becomes the circumference of the tube it rolls into, and compare volumes through the quantity that is conserved. Sort before taking a median, and say why one large value moves the mean and not the median. Count the complement in a small sample space rather than the event itself. Run a recurrence backwards and check it forwards. And estimate the size of an answer before computing its digits, so that a factor-of-ten slip is caught in five seconds.

I read the four syllabus headings as five computations: a share of one base, a multiplication chain, a logarithm identity, a length-to-volume conversion, and a count of outcomes. Every question in this section, including the six previous-year items worked below, is one of the five.

  • Analytical aptitude — the neighbouring General Aptitude lesson, which owns series by difference table and the logic half of the section. This lesson owns the arithmetic half.

The five computations, in one line each

  • Share. Name the base, then divide the part by it.
  • Chain. Percent times total, divided by a rate, with the units cancelled.
  • Identity. A product becomes a sum; a power becomes a factor.
  • Shape. Find what is conserved, then use the right power of the length.
  • Count. Sort before a median; count the complement before a probability.

Intuition first: a shopkeeper's three columns

A shopkeeper who sells three kinds of coin does not add up the numbers of coins and call it money. She keeps three columns: how many, times what each is worth, equals what it comes to. Only the third column can be added, and only the third column can be compared. Educational interpretation Every question in this section is that ledger with a different third column. In a data question the third column is grams, reached through kilocalories. In a mensuration question it is a volume, reached through a length. In a probability question it is a count of outcomes. The habit worth building is to write down what the third column is called before doing any arithmetic, because the commonest wrong answer is the right arithmetic in the wrong column.

Why this is worth a lesson rather than a formula list: there are four syllabus headings and about a dozen formulas, and no question ever needs more than two of them. What the section actually rewards is the habit of naming the base, naming the unit, and checking the answer's size.

Ratio, proportion and the base

A ratio a : b compares two counts of the same kind. A ratio says nothing about amounts until each term is multiplied by the value of one unit. A proportion is an equation between two ratios, and it is what lets you carry a relation from one pair to another.

Mathematical derivation A percentage is a ratio whose second term has been fixed at 100. So "15 of 150 is 10 per cent" and "15/150 = 10/100" are the same sentence. The consequence is the one thing to remember: a percentage is meaningless without the total it is a percentage of. Two percentages of two different totals cannot be compared, subtracted or averaged until both are converted to a common base.

Percentage points, and per cent change

A change from 20 per cent to 25 per cent is five percentage points and a 25 per cent increase, because 25/20 = 1.25. Both sentences describe the same data and neither is wrong; a question that offers both as options is testing whether you can say which one it asked for.

change per cent = (new − old) / old × 100The denominator is the OLD value. Dividing by the new value gives the reverse change, which is a smaller number and is the usual planted distractor.

Mathematical derivation Successive changes multiply rather than add: a rise of 10 per cent and a fall of 10 per cent leaves 0.99 of the original, not 1. That is the same compounding fact that scene 9 uses for growth over time.

Data interpretation is a chain, not a reading

A chart gives shares of a stated total. A table gives a rate. Neither is an amount. The amount is the product of the share and the total, and the count is that amount divided by the rate.

grams = (per cent / 100) × total ÷ (units per gram)Write the three units in a row — per cent of kcal, kcal, grams. If the last unit is the one the question asked for, the chain is right. If not, no amount of arithmetic will rescue it.

Mathematical derivation A pie chart's sector for a share p per cent has angle 3.6p degrees, because 360/100 = 3.6. The five sectors in the worked example therefore close at exactly 360 degrees, which is the page's check that the shares were read correctly.

The two logarithm rules, and nothing else

A logarithm answers the question "to what power must the base be raised". Everything this paper needs follows from two rules.

log(ab) = log a + log b
log(a^k) = k log a
log(a/b) = log a − log b (a consequence)A product becomes a sum; a power becomes a factor; a quotient becomes a difference. All logarithms in one equation must have the same base, and every argument must be positive.

Mathematical derivation There is no rule for log(a + b). A condition like log(p² + q²) = log p + log q + 2 log 3 is therefore solved by combining the right side into one logarithm — log p + log q is log pq, and 2 log 3 is log 9 — and then dropping the logarithm from both sides, because equal logarithms of positive numbers mean equal numbers.

Mensuration: find what is conserved

Rolling a sheet does not change its area, and does not preserve its perimeter or its volume. In a comparison question, the conserved quantity is the bridge between the two objects.

area of a rectangle = l × w
area of a cube's surface = 6a²
volume of a cylinder = π r² h, with r = circumference / 2π
volume of a cube = a³A length to an area squares; a length to a volume cubes. Scaling a shape by k multiplies area by k² and volume by k³ — the single most useful scaling fact in the section.

Mathematical derivation Which edge becomes the circumference is decided by which pair of edges is joined. Joining the two longer edges puts the seam along the length, so the length is the height and the short edge is the distance around. That is the whole difficulty of the worked example, and getting it backwards changes the answer by a factor of thirteen and a half.

Statistics: three centres, three questions

Mean is the sum divided by the count. Median is the middle of the sorted list, averaging the two central values when the count is even. Mode is the most frequent value. They answer different questions, and a single large value separates them.

median: sort, then take position (n+1)/2
mean = Σx / n
one large value moves the mean, not the medianThe median is resistant: it depends on rank, not on magnitude, so changing 69 to 690 leaves it unchanged. The mean is not resistant: it is a sum, so one large value moves it.

Mathematical derivation One statement about the median is true of every sample: at most half the values lie strictly above it. The same sentence with "mean" in place of "median" is false, and the sample 0, 10, 10 refutes it — the mean is 6.67 and two of the three values exceed it. GATE CSE 2023 GA Question 4 asked for exactly the true version.

Probability: a sample space, and its complement

For equally likely outcomes, the probability of an event is the number of outcomes in it divided by the number in the whole space. That definition is all this section needs.

P(E) = |E| / |S|
P(at least one) = 1 − P(none)The complement rule works because "at least one" and "none" are exhaustive (one of them always happens) and disjoint (never both). Subtracting from one is legal only under those two conditions.

Mathematical derivation Without replacement changes the space: two draws from five red and three blue give 28 unordered pairs, of which 3 are both blue, so at least one red is 25/28. With replacement the draws are independent and the same question gives 1 − (3/8)² = 55/64. Same words, different space, different answer.

Series: a recurrence is not a pattern

A recurrence is given in the question: each term is defined from earlier ones. Then nothing has to be guessed, and the relation can be run in either direction — the term two below is the upper term minus the lower one. A pattern is not given, and then a difference table names a relation that fits the terms you can see; six terms never determine a seventh, which is why the analytical-aptitude lesson treats that case as a convention fixed by the options.

Mathematical derivation Going backwards is exact here because the relation F(n+1) = F(n) + F(n−1) determines each term from the other two, so the map from consecutive pairs to consecutive pairs is invertible. That is why 37 and 60 pin the whole sequence, in both directions, with no assumption about a closed form.

Six previous-year items, in the order the five computations should be applied

Example 1 — a ratio, turned into money

GATE CSE 2024 Set 1, GA Question 4 (one mark). The numbers of Rs 1, Rs 5 and Rs 10 coins are in the ratio 5 : 3 : 13. What percentage of the total money is in Rs 5 coins?

Step 1. Values: 5 × 1 = 5, 3 × 5 = 15, 13 × 10 = 130.

Step 2. Total value 150. That is the base — not the 21 coins.

Mathematical derivation 15/150 = 1/10 = 10 per cent. Verified fact The official key for this paper marks option C, which is the ten per cent option. The page also computes the two wrong bases: 3/21 = 14 2/7 per cent, from a count over a count, and 15/50 = 30 per cent, from a part over the wrong part.

Example 2 — a chart joined to a table

GATE CSE 2024 Set 1, GA Question 8 (two marks). A pie chart gives the share of a 2,000 kcal diet: carbohydrates 35 per cent, proteins 20, unsaturated fat 20, saturated fat 20, trans fat 5. A table gives energy densities: 4 kcal/g for carbohydrate and protein, 9 kcal/g for each fat. How many grams of fat in total?

Step 1. Fats are 20 + 20 + 5 = 45 per cent of 2,000 = 900 kcal.

Mathematical derivation All three fats have the same density, so 900 ÷ 9 = 100 grams exactly. Verified fact The official key marks option C, 100. The page checks the sector angles close at 360 degrees and that the five energy figures sum to 2,000 kcal, so the reading of the chart is verified rather than assumed.

Example 3 — a logarithm, then algebra

GATE CSE 2024 Set 1, GA Question 5 (one mark). For positive non-zero reals p and q, if log(p² + q²) = log p + log q + 2 log 3, find (p⁴ + q⁴)/(p²q²).

Step 1. Right side: log p + log q = log pq, and 2 log 3 = log 9, so the condition is log(p² + q²) = log(9pq).

Step 2. Drop the logarithm: p² + q² = 9pq, so p/q + q/p = 9.

Mathematical derivation Square it: (p² + q²)² = p⁴ + 2p²q² + q⁴ = 81p²q², so p⁴ + q⁴ = 79p²q² and the ratio is 79. Verified fact The official key marks option A. The page confirms the value numerically at both roots of r² − 9r + 1 = 0, so the answer does not depend on which root you take.

Example 4 — a sheet, a tube and a cube

GATE CSE 2024 Set 1, GA Question 7 (two marks). A sheet 54 cm × 4 cm is rolled by joining its two longer edges into a tube. A cube is taken whose surface area equals the sheet's area. Find the ratio of the tube's volume to the cube's volume.

Step 1. Sheet area 216. Cube: 6a² = 216 gives a = 6 and volume 216.

Step 2. Joining the two 54 cm edges puts the seam along the length: height 54, circumference 4, so r = 4/(2π) = 2/π.

Mathematical derivation Tube volume π(2/π)² × 54 = 216/π. Ratio = (216/π)/216 = 1/π. Verified fact The official key marks option A. The page also computes the other reading of the fold — joining the two 4 cm edges gives 13.5/π, about 4.30 — and the reading error that uses the circumference as a radius, which gives 4/π and is an offered option.

Example 5 — a median, and one outlier

GATE CSE 2024 Set 1, GA Question 3 (one mark). The sample is 9, 18, 11, 14, 15, 17, 10, 69, 11, 13. Find the median.

Step 1. Sorted: 9, 10, 11, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 69.

Mathematical derivation Ten values, so the median is the average of the fifth and sixth: (13 + 14)/2 = 13.5. Verified fact The official key marks option A, which is this value. The page computes the mean as well: 187/10 = 18.7, above the median because 69 is a large value. Removing 69 drops the mean to about 13.11 and leaves the median at 13 — the definition of resistance.

Example 6 — a recurrence, run backwards

GATE CSE 2023, GA Question 3 (one mark). A series of natural numbers obeys F(n+1) = F(n) + F(n−1) for all n ≥ 2. If F6 = 37 and F7 = 60, what is F1?

Steps. F5 = 60 − 37 = 23. F4 = 37 − 23 = 14. F3 = 23 − 14 = 9. F2 = 14 − 9 = 5. F1 = 9 − 5 = 4.

Mathematical derivation F1 = 4. Verified fact The official key for GATE CSE 2023 marks option A for this question. The page runs the sequence forward from 4 and 5 and gets 9, 14, 23, 37, 60 — both given terms reproduced exactly, which is the check that makes a backwards derivation safe.

The boundaries: where each computation stops being safe

A formula applied outside its condition is a trap. Each card names the condition and says what happens when it fails — and, where possible, gives the size of the error.

Which edge becomes the circumference

Joining the two longer edges of a 54 × 4 sheet makes 54 the height and 4 the distance around. Joining the two shorter edges makes 4 the height and 54 the distance around.

Mathematical derivation Both readings conserve area — 4 × 54 = 216 either way — so the conservation check does not distinguish them. They differ by a factor of 13.5: 1/π against 13.5/π, that is 0.318 against 4.297. The page computes both, which is why the wording of the question, and not the arithmetic, decides the answer.

An even count has no middle element

With ten values there is no position 5.5 to read. The median is defined as the average of the two central values, so it need not be one of the data values at all — 13.5 is not in the sample.

Mathematical derivation The position of the median is (n+1)/2 = 5.5 in units of rank, which is exactly why an average is needed. Taking the fifth value and stopping gives 13, one of the planted options in a paper of this kind.

The base can change halfway through a question

"Boys were 65 per cent of those who appeared; girls were 60 per cent of those who qualified" contains two different bases. Comparing 65 with 60 is comparing two fractions of two different totals.

Mathematical derivation The fix is to express both against one base. Girls took 60 per cent of the qualified places, so boys took 40, and 40 < 60 — fewer boys qualified. That is GATE CSE 2022 GA Question 7, worked in the analytical-aptitude lesson.

Grouping is exact only when the rate is shared

Adding the three fat percentages and dividing once by 9 is exact because all three fats have the same density. If unsaturated fat were 9 kcal/g and trans fat 4, the single division would be wrong.

Mathematical derivation The page checks this explicitly: it computes the grouped answer and the per-row sum and asserts they agree, which they do only because the densities are equal. When they are not, divide per row and add the grams.

With replacement or without

Two draws from five red and three blue give 25/28 for at least one red without replacement, and 55/64 with replacement. The words "drawn one by one and put back" change the sample space, and therefore the answer.

Mathematical derivation 25/28 ≈ 0.893 and 55/64 ≈ 0.859. Close enough to look like rounding, far enough to be a different option. The page enumerates the 28 unordered pairs rather than trusting the binomial coefficient.

Estimation has a range where it is honest

2¹⁰ = 1024, so calling it 1000 is a 2.4 per cent error. Ten of those in a row compounds to about 27 per cent at 2¹⁰⁰, which is fine for choosing an option and useless for a two-decimal answer.

Mathematical derivation The rule of seventy-two is tuned near eight per cent growth. At one per cent a year it says 72 years and the truth is about 69; at twenty per cent it says 3.6 and the truth is about 3.8. Use it to eliminate options, then compute exactly if two survive.

A counterexample to "at most half are above the mean"

The statement is true of the median and false of the mean. The smallest counterexample is the sample 0, 10, 10.

Mathematical derivation The mean is 20/3 = 6.67, and two of the three values — 10 and 10 — lie strictly above it. So two thirds of the sample exceed the mean, which is more than half. The median of the same sample is 10, and only zero values lie above it. This is exactly the distinction GATE CSE 2023 GA Question 4 tested, and it is why the page computes both counts rather than stating the theorem.

Six beliefs that cost marks, and the smallest thing that corrects each

Each entry gives the belief as it is usually held, the correction, and a counterexample small enough to check in the exam.

1. "A ratio is an amount."

Correction. A ratio compares counts. It becomes an amount only after each term is multiplied by the value of one unit.

Counterexample. Rs 1, Rs 5 and Rs 10 coins in the ratio 5 : 3 : 13 hold 3.3, 10 and 86.7 per cent of the money, not 24, 14 and 62 per cent of it. Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q4, key C.

2. "Two percentages can be compared."

Correction. Only against the same base. A percentage is a fraction of a stated total, and a fraction of a different total is a different kind of number.

Counterexample. 65 per cent of those who appeared were boys, 60 per cent of those who qualified were girls, and fewer boys qualified — 40 per cent of the qualified places. Verified fact GATE CSE 2022 GA Q7, key D.

3. "log(a + b) = log a + log b."

Correction. The rule is for products. For a sum there is no rule, and the expression usually cannot be simplified at all.

Counterexample. log(1 + 1) = log 2 ≈ 0.301, while log 1 + log 1 = 0. Mathematical derivation This is why the worked logarithm question combines the right-hand side first: the only way to remove a logarithm is to have one logarithm on each side of an equality.

4. "Squaring a sum of two squares gives a sum of two squares."

Correction. The square of p² + q² has three terms, and the middle one is 2p²q².

Counterexample. Dropping it turns 79 into 81, which is exactly the gap between the correct option and a plausible wrong one in the paper. Mathematical derivation (p² + q²)² = 81p²q² gives p⁴ + q⁴ = 81p²q² − 2p²q² = 79p²q².

5. "Mean and median are two words for average."

Correction. They answer different questions. The mean is a sum over a count; the median is a position in a sorted list. One large value moves the first and not the second.

Counterexample. In 9, 18, 11, 14, 15, 17, 10, 69, 11, 13 the mean is 18.7 and the median is 13.5. Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q3 asks for the median precisely because the mean is the tempting wrong answer.

6. "At least one means add the chances."

Correction. Adding three chances of one half gives three halves, which is not a probability. "At least one" is the complement of "none", and the complement is subtracted from one.

Counterexample. Three fair tosses: the chance of at least one head is 7/8, not 3/2. Mathematical derivation The page enumerates all eight outcomes and counts seven, so the number is counted rather than argued.

Closely confused pairs, side by side

Percentage points versus per cent. 20 to 25 is five points and a 25 per cent rise. A question that offers both is asking which noun it used.

Circumference versus radius. The distance around a tube is 2πr, so a circumference of 4 means r = 2/π. Using 4 as the radius multiplies the volume by four, and 4/π is offered in the paper as an option.

Surface area versus volume. A cube of surface area 216 has side 6 and volume 216, because 6² = 6 only at 6. At any other side the two numbers differ, so "equal surface area" and "equal volume" are not the same condition.

Recurrence versus pattern. A recurrence is given and can be run either way; a pattern is inferred and fixes only the terms you can see. The analytical lesson shows six terms failing to determine a seventh.

Simple versus compound growth. Five per cent for fourteen years is 1.70 simple and 1.98 compounded. The difference is not rounding; it is the difference between adding a rate and multiplying by a factor.

How this section is actually asked

What follows describes the shapes these questions take in the papers named, with the source for each. The paper does not label its questions by syllabus heading, so the grouping here is mine and rests on what each question tests. Nothing below is a forecast of the 2027 paper.

Shape 1 — the ratio turned into a share

Two or three quantities in a ratio, each with a different unit value, and a question about money, weight or time rather than counts. The work is one multiplication per part and one division by the value total.

Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q4, one mark, official key C — the coin question worked on the Examples tab. Community observation An archive files several 2021–2025 items of this shape under quantitative aptitude; their texts were not read from the papers and are not quoted here.

Shape 2 — the chart joined to a table

A pie chart or bar chart of shares, plus a table of rates, and a question that needs both. The work is the chain share × total ÷ rate, with the units checked.

Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q8, two marks, official key C — 100 grams of fat. Community observation An archive also files GATE CSE 2024 Set 2 GA Q8 (two pie charts of electricity generation, 2007 against 2023) and GATE CSE 2017 Set 2 GA Q65 (an air-pressure contour map) under data interpretation. The figures are not reproduced here; the pattern is what is being recorded.

Shape 3 — the identity that removes the unknown

An equation with logarithms, exponents or powers, where one identity collapses it. The work is to combine each side into one logarithm or one power, then compare.

Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q5, one mark, official key A — the answer 79. Community observation An archive files a logarithmic item from GATE CSE 2012 (AR paper, GA-6) of the same shape; it is not a CS paper and is not quoted.

Shape 4 — the shape that is rolled, filled or scaled

A rectangle rolled into a tube, a cube compared with it, or a figure scaled. The work is the conserved quantity, then the right power of the length, then one ratio.

Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q7, two marks, official key A — the ratio 1/π. Community observation The same paper's Q9 folds a 20 cm × 8 cm sheet three times and asks for a perimeter; that item is worked in the spatial-aptitude lesson, because the operation is folding rather than mensuration.

Shape 5 — the centre of a list, and the count of a space

A short sample with one outlier, and a question about mean, median or mode; or a small sample space and a question about a compound event. The work is a sort, or an enumeration.

Verified fact GATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q3, one mark, official key A — the median 13.5. GATE CSE 2023 GA Q4 asked which statement about mean and median must be true, and the true one is the median statement. Verified fact GATE CSE 2023 GA Q3, one mark, official key A — the Fibonacci-type recurrence, F1 = 4, which is the series shape rather than the statistics shape.

What to expect, stated carefully

Two things are worth saying plainly. First, the ten General Aptitude questions are split across four headings, so quantitative aptitude is typically three or four of them, worth five to eight marks — a community tagging of past papers, not an official figure and not a promise. Second, the recent papers lean toward questions where a figure or a table carries the data: four of the six items worked above need a chart, a table or a diagram. Pure formula recall is rare; joining two sources of data is common.

The practical consequence is that the chain habit matters more than any single formula. A candidate who writes the three units down before computing will beat a candidate who has memorised more formulas, because the errors in this section are almost all unit errors and base errors rather than arithmetic errors.

MCQGATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q4

The number of coins of Rs 1, Rs 5 and Rs 10 denominations that a person has are in the ratio 5 : 3 : 13. Of the total amount, the percentage of money in Rs 5 coins is

  1. 21%
  2. 14 2/7%
  3. 10%
  4. 30%

Answer: C — 10%

Values are 5 × 1 = 5, 3 × 5 = 15, 13 × 10 = 130, so the total is 150 and the Rs 5 share is 15/150 = 10 per cent.

Why B appears. 3/21 is a count divided by a count: 14 2/7 per cent. The base is money, not coins. Why D appears. 15/50 divides the part by the wrong part. Why A appears. 21 is the total number of coins, and it has no place in a money question.

Worth knowing. The page computes all three bases, so you can see that each distractor is a real calculation on a wrong base rather than a random number.

MCQGATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q8

A pie chart gives the share of a 2,000 kcal diet: carbohydrates 35%, proteins 20%, unsaturated fat 20%, saturated fat 20%, trans fat 5%. Energy densities are 4 kcal/g for carbohydrate and protein and 9 kcal/g for each fat. The total fat, in grams, is

  1. 44.4
  2. 77.8
  3. 100
  4. 3,600

Answer: C — 100

Fats are 20 + 20 + 5 = 45 per cent of 2,000 = 900 kcal. All three have density 9, so 900/9 = 100 g.

Why A appears. 400/9 = 44.4 is one fat alone — the answer to a different question. Why B appears. Two fats added, not three. Why D appears. 3,600 has no unit at all; it is 2,000 × 1.8, a number with no meaning here.

The trap. The question says total fat, so all three rows must be included. Read the noun the question uses.

MCQGATE CSE 2024 Set 1 GA Q7

A rectangular paper sheet of dimensions 54 cm × 4 cm is taken. The two longer edges of the sheet are joined together to create a cylindrical tube. A cube whose surface area is equal to the area of the sheet is also taken. The ratio of the volume of the cylindrical tube to the volume of the cube is

  1. 1/π
  2. 2/π
  3. 3/π
  4. 4/π

Answer: A — 1/π

Area 216, so the cube has side 6 and volume 216. Joining the two 54 cm edges gives height 54 and circumference 4, so r = 2/π and the tube is π(2/π)² × 54 = 216/π. The ratio is 1/π.

Why D appears. Using the circumference 4 as if it were the radius gives four times the volume, that is 4/π. Why B and C appear. Intermediate arithmetic on a partly corrected radius.

The trap. The two longer edges are the 54 cm edges, so 54 becomes the height. Joining the shorter pair gives 13.5/π, which is not offered — the option set confirms the reading.

MCQGATE CSE 2023 GA Q3

A series of natural numbers F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7 obeys F(n+1) = F(n) + F(n−1) for all integers n ≥ 2. If F6 = 37 and F7 = 60, then F1 is

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 9
  4. 14

Answer: A — 4

F5 = 60 − 37 = 23, F4 = 37 − 23 = 14, F3 = 23 − 14 = 9, F2 = 14 − 9 = 5, F1 = 9 − 5 = 4.

Why B appears. 5 is F2, not F1: an off-by-one from stopping a step early. Why C and D appear. They are F3 and F4, the terms you meet on the way down.

The check. Run forward from 4 and 5: 9, 14, 23, 37, 60. Both given terms come out exactly, which is what makes the backwards derivation safe.

NATOriginal practice

A price rises by 25 per cent and then falls by 20 per cent. What is the net change, in per cent? Give a signed number, using a negative sign for a fall.

Answer: 0

1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00, so the price returns to where it started. The two changes multiply; they do not add.

Why +5 appears. Adding 25 and −20 as if percentages were points on a line. Why −5 appears. Treating the 20 per cent fall as 20 per cent of the new price and then subtracting in the wrong order.

The rule. Successive percentage changes multiply as factors. A rise of a then a fall of b leaves (1+a)(1−b) of the original.

NATOriginal practice

A cube is painted and then cut into 27 equal smaller cubes. How many of the small cubes have exactly two painted faces?

Answer: 12

A 3 × 3 × 3 cut has 8 corner cubes with three painted faces, 12 edge cubes with exactly two, 6 face centres with one, and 1 hidden cube with none. 8 + 12 + 6 + 1 = 27.

Why 8 appears. That is the corner count. Why 6 appears. That is the face-centre count. Why 24 appears. Counting each edge twice.

The rule. On an n × n × n cut: corners 8, edges 12(n−2), faces 6(n−2)², interior (n−2)³. Check the four counts sum to n³ — here 8 + 12 + 6 + 1 = 27.

NATOriginal practice

A bag holds 5 red and 3 blue balls. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is red? Give the answer as a fraction in lowest terms, written as a/b.

Answer: 25/28

There are 28 unordered pairs. Both blue happens in 3 of them, so at least one red is 1 − 3/28 = 25/28.

Why 5/8 appears. That is the chance that the first draw is red, which answers a different question. Why 25/64 appears. That is the with-replacement answer for a different counting method. Why 55/64 appears. That is the correct with-replacement answer: 1 − (3/8)².

The rule. Without replacement the space is pairs; with replacement it is ordered pairs. The page enumerates all 28 pairs rather than trusting the formula.

NATOriginal practice

Using the approximation that ten doublings are about three decimal digits, estimate the number of decimal digits in 2¹⁰⁰. Give a whole number.

Answer: 31

2¹⁰⁰ = (2¹⁰)¹⁰ ≈ (10³)¹⁰ = 10³⁰, which has 31 digits — a number of the form 10³⁰ has 31 digits, not 30. The exact value is about 1.268 × 10³⁰.

Why 30 appears. Reading 10³⁰ as "thirty digits". The exponent counts zeros after the leading digit, so the digit count is one more. Why 33 appears. Using 2¹⁰ ≈ 10³ without compounding the 2.4 per cent error, which over ten factors is about 27 per cent — not a factor of a thousand.

The rule. log₁₀(2) ≈ 0.30103, so 2¹⁰⁰ ≈ 10^30.103, that is 1.27 × 10³⁰, and a number of that size has 31 digits.

Revision sheet — the whole section on one screen

Formulas, decision procedures and traps, in the order you would want them in the last hour before the paper.

Formulas

per cent = part / base × 100
change per cent = (new − old) / old × 100
successive: (1+a)(1+b) as factors
grams = per cent/100 × total ÷ density
log(ab) = log a + log b ; log(a^k) = k log a
cube surface 6a² ; cylinder πr²h, r = C/2π
scaling: area ×k², volume ×k³
median at rank (n+1)/2 ; mean = Σx/n
P(at least one) = 1 − P(none)
doubling time ≈ 72 / rate per cent

Each of these is derived in the Build-up or shown in the animation, and each is used on one previous-year item on the Examples tab.

Decision procedures

  • Ratio question. Multiply each share by its unit value, add to get the base, divide the part by the base.
  • Data question. Share × total ÷ rate, with the three units written in a row.
  • Log question. Combine each side into one logarithm, drop the logarithms, then do algebra.
  • Shape question. Find the conserved quantity, decide which length is the circumference, then take the right power.
  • Statistics question. Sort first; use rank for the median and the sum for the mean; say which the question named.
  • Probability question. Write the space, count the complement, check that the two counts add to the size of the space.
  • Series question. If a recurrence is given, run it backwards and then forwards; if not, use a difference table.

Traps, in one line each

  • A ratio is counts; money needs a unit value.
  • The base is the total the percentage is of — not the count, not the wrong part.
  • Two percentages of different totals are not comparable.
  • log of a sum does not split, and 2 log 3 is log 9.
  • Squaring p² + q² keeps the middle term.
  • A circumference is not a radius, and a diameter is not a radius.
  • An even sample's median averages two values and may not be in the data.
  • "At most half above" is true of the median and false of the mean.
  • Without replacement changes the space; with replacement gives 55/64, not 25/28.
  • 5 is F2, not F1: check a backwards series forwards.

Three questions to ask before answering anything here

What is the base, and what is it a base of? What is the unit of the answer I am computing, and does it match the unit the question asked for? And is the answer the size I expected — within a factor of ten, before I trust the digits?

Those three questions settle more of this section than any amount of practice volume. The rest of the library covers the machinery: counting for the binomial coefficients, probability and random variables for the formal treatment, analytical aptitude for the series-by-difference-table tool, and spatial aptitude for folding and symmetry, which is where the paper's paper-folding questions belong.
