> Caption: The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms, and the logarithm of a power is the exponent times the logarithm.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *log of a sum does not split. There is no rule for log(a plus b).*

> Caption: The condition is log of p squared plus q squared, equals log p plus log q plus two log three.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Two log three is log nine, not log six. The coefficient is an exponent, not a multiplier.*

**1:45–1:52** — Exponentiate both sides, and the logarithms disappear. p squared plus q squared equals nine p q. Divide by p q and the condition becomes p over q plus q over p equals nine.

> Caption: Exponentiate both sides: p squared plus q squared equals nine times p q.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *This step needs both sides positive. A logarithm of zero or of a negative number does not exist, so the paper says non-zero real variables.*

**1:52–2:00** — Square the relation. The left side gains a middle term, two p squared q squared, and the right becomes eighty-one p squared q squared. Subtract the middle term and the ratio is seventy-nine. The official key is A.

> Caption: Then p to the fourth plus q to the fourth is eighty-one minus two, so seventy-nine times p squared q squared. The answer is seventy-nine.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Squaring p squared plus q squared gives three terms. Dropping the middle one produces eighty-one, which is not the answer.*
