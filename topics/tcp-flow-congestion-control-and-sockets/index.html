Topic and objective

TOPIC: TCP flow control, congestion control and the socket API — one clause of GATE 2027 Section 10, Computer Networks.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE: Separate the two limits on a TCP sender — the advertised receiver window and the sender's own congestion window — and compute the smaller one. Walk a congestion window round trip by round trip through slow start and congestion avoidance, applying the correct rule below and at the slow-start threshold. Apply the two loss signals correctly, and say why a timeout and three duplicate acknowledgements give different answers from the same numbers. Turn a sawtooth into an average throughput, and compute the window a path needs from its bandwidth-delay product. Finally, order the socket calls on each side, map them onto the three-way handshake, and size a segment from the link MTU.

I read this clause as two windows and two signals. Flow control is a budget the receiver publishes; congestion control is a rate the sender infers from loss. Everything a GATE question asks here — a window at round trip k, a time to reach a target, an average throughput, a socket call order — is one of those two ideas evaluated on a small table.

Prerequisites

  • Layering, switching and performance metrics — delay, bandwidth, round trip, and the throughput arithmetic this lesson reuses.
  • IP fragmentation, CIDR and NAT — the IPv4 header, and therefore where the 20-byte overhead in MSS comes from.
  • Arithmetic you already have: doubling, halving, ceilings, and converting between bytes, bits and time.

Nothing here needs the routing lesson. A question may quote a round trip time without saying how it was measured; treat it as given.

The five ideas, in one line each

  • Two windows. In flight ≤ min(receiver window, congestion window). The smaller one binds and names the limit.
  • Flow control. Usable window = advertised window − bytes in flight. Signalled in every ACK, in bytes.
  • Slow start. +1 MSS per ACK, so a doubling per round trip, until the threshold.
  • Congestion avoidance. +1 MSS per round trip; a loss halves the threshold, and the signal decides what the window becomes.
  • The socket API. Server: socket, bind, listen, accept. Client: socket, connect. The kernel runs the handshake underneath.

Intuition first: a counter with a shelf and a road

Picture a shop that sends parcels down a road. Two things can stop the parcels. The shop's counter has shelf space — once the shelf is full, no more parcels can be handed over, however empty the road is. And the road itself can jam — once too many vans are out there, everything slows and some are lost, however much shelf space remains.

The shopkeeper keeps two numbers: how much shelf space the customer has left (the customer tells them this with every delivery note), and how many vans they believe the road can take (nobody tells them; they learn it by watching for losses). The number of vans in motion is the smaller of the two. Educational interpretation The analogy carries the structure — two independent limits, one published and one inferred — but not the timing details, so do not read any number out of it.

Formal vocabulary, every symbol in words

MSS — maximum segment size: the largest amount of application data one TCP segment carries, in bytes.

Bytes in flight — bytes sent whose receipt has not yet been acknowledged. Equivalently: last byte sent minus last byte acknowledged.

rwnd — the receive window, or advertised window. A 16-bit field in every segment header, in bytes, saying how much more data the receiver is willing to buffer. This is the flow-control limit.

cwnd — the congestion window, in bytes or in segments. A sender-side variable, never transmitted, standing for what the sender believes the network can carry. This is the congestion-control limit.

ssthresh — the slow-start threshold. The value of cwnd at which the sender stops doubling and starts adding one segment per round trip.

The two rules of the sender

Rule 1, the cap. The sender may have at most $$\min(\text{rwnd},\ \text{cwnd})$$ bytes in flight. Nothing else limits it.

Rule 2, the budget. The usable window right now is the advertised window minus what is already in flight: $$\text{usable} = \text{rwnd} - (\text{lastByteSent} - \text{lastByteAcked}).$$

Verified fact Both rules are the standard statement of flow control: the receive window bounds outstanding data, and the sender must not reuse window space until it is acknowledged. RFC 9293 (TCP) states the window as a byte count measured from the acknowledgement number.

Slow start, stated exactly

Verified fact In slow start, each incoming acknowledgement increases cwnd by at most one segment: cwnd grows by $$\min(N,\ \text{SMSS})$$ where N is the number of newly acknowledged bytes. Because a whole window is acknowledged per round trip, cwnd approximately doubles per round trip.

Starting at one segment the series is 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 … in segments. In bytes with a 2 KB segment it is 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 KB.

Verified fact RFC 5681 also allows an initial window of up to four segments. GATE questions state a start of one segment; use the number the question gives, and do not import the RFC's initial window into an exam trace.

Congestion avoidance, and the two cuts

Verified fact In congestion avoidance cwnd increases by about one segment per round trip — per acknowledgement the increase is $$\text{SMSS} \times \text{SMSS} / \text{cwnd}$$ so that a full window of acknowledgements adds one segment.

On a timeout: $$\text{ssthresh} = \max(\text{flight}/2,\ 2\ \text{SMSS}),\qquad \text{cwnd} = 1\ \text{SMSS}.$$ The connection restarts in slow start and doubles up to the new threshold.

On three duplicate ACKs: the missing segment is retransmitted immediately without waiting for the timer. In the Reno variant $$\text{cwnd} = \text{ssthresh}$$ and growth continues linearly; in Tahoe cwnd restarts at one segment. Exam questions either name the variant or state the rule to use.

The formula that turns the sawtooth into a number

The window climbs linearly from half its peak to the peak and is then cut back to half. Its time average is therefore three quarters of the peak, so the average rate is

average rate = 0.75 × W_max / RTTW_max is the peak congestion window, in bytes if the answer is to be bytes per second; RTT is one round trip. Divide by 8 for bytes and multiply by 8 for bits — decide which the question wants before the last step.

The bandwidth-delay product is the companion quantity: $$\text{BDP} = \text{bandwidth} \times \text{RTT}$$ is the data the path can hold in flight, so a window smaller than the BDP leaves the link idle. Four worked traces, each with its arithmetic shown

Example 1 — the usable window, one step

A receiver advertises 6 MSS and 2 MSS are unacknowledged.

  1. Usable = 6 − 2 = 4 MSS. Rule 2: the window is a byte count, and acknowledged bytes free it.
  2. If cwnd were 3 MSS, the sender could still send only 3. Rule 1: the smaller window binds.

The connection is congestion limited in the second case and flow limited in the first, and the name comes from which window is smaller, never from which one changed.

Example 2 — GATE 2014 Set 1 Q27, fully walked

cwnd is 32 KB at a timeout, MSS 2 KB, RTT 100 ms. How long to get back to 32 KB?

  1. Convert: 32 KB ÷ 2 KB = 16 MSS.
  2. Timeout: ssthresh = 16 ÷ 2 = 8, cwnd = 1. Halve the flight, restart at one segment.
  3. Slow start below 8: 1, 2, 4, 8 — reached at round trip 3, i.e. after four round trips of transmission.
  4. Congestion avoidance from 8: 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 — eight more round trips.
  5. 16 MSS is reached at round trip 11, so 11 × 100 ms = 1100 ms.

Trap. Doubling to 16 directly would give 4 round trips and 400 ms: that ignores the threshold and the linear phase. The published key accepts a range because the round trip containing the timeout may or may not be counted; the trace here counts from the restart and gives 1100 ms.

Example 3 — GATE 2020 Q55, ten round trips

RTT 6 ms, rwnd 50 KB, ssthresh 32 KB, MSS 2 KB, no losses. cwnd at t + 60 ms?

  1. 60 ms ÷ 6 ms = 10 round trips.
  2. Doubling in KB: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 — the threshold is reached after four round trips.
  3. Linear at 2 KB per round trip: 34, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44 — six more round trips.
  4. 44 KB is below 50 KB, so rwnd never bound. Answer 44 KB.

Trap. Inventing a threshold by halving rwnd (25 KB) changes every line after round trip 5. The threshold here is stated in the question.

Example 4 — GATE 2024 Set 2 Q54, both signals

cwnd is 12 MSS at a loss, and the next two round trips are clean.

  1. ssthresh = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 in both cases.
  2. Timeout: cwnd = 1, then 2, then 4. Answer 4 MSS.
  3. Three duplicate ACKs, Reno: cwnd = 6, then 7, then 8. Answer 8 MSS.
  4. A third clean round trip after the timeout gives 6, not 8: doubling stops at the threshold.

Takeaway. The same numbers produce 4 or 8 depending on one word in the question. The loss signal is part of the given data, not a detail.

Example 5 — throughput and the window the path needs

Peak window 100 MSS, MSS 1460 B, RTT 40 ms.

  1. Average window = 0.75 × 100 = 75 MSS. The sawtooth spends its time between half the peak and the peak.
  2. 75 × 1460 = 109500 bytes per round trip.
  3. 109500 ÷ 0.04 s = 2737500 B/s = 21.9 Mbps.
  4. BDP = 100 Mbps × 0.1 s = 1.25 MB, which needs a window of 857 segments — far more than a 16-bit field names, so window scaling is required.

Trap. Using the peak window instead of the average overstates throughput by a third (100/75). Using bytes per second as bits per second understates it by eight.

Example 6 — socket calls and the segment size

A server and a client on Ethernet, MTU 1500, transferring 10000 bytes.

  1. Server: socket → bind → listen → accept → recv/send → close.
  2. Client: socket → connect → send/recv → close. connect sends the SYN.
  3. MSS = 1500 − 20 − 20 = 1460 bytes. With IPv6: 1500 − 40 − 20 = 1440.
  4. 10000 bytes = 6 × 1460 + 1240, so seven segments and a 1240-byte tail.

Note. accept returns a new socket for the connection; the listening socket stays listening. The handshake is completed by the kernel before accept returns.

Where the rules stop being enough

Each card names a boundary case, the condition that makes it a boundary case, and what the correct behaviour is. Read these before the practice set: every one of them has been the subject of a real question somewhere in this clause.

Zero window

Condition: the receiver buffer is full, so the advertised window is 0.

The sender must not send data, but it must keep probing, because the window update that reopens the connection can be lost. Without a probe the connection deadlocks with both sides waiting.

A zero window is a pause, not a close. Only FIN or RST ends the connection.

A loss at the end of a window: no duplicate ACKs

Condition: the last segment of the window is lost and no further data is in flight.

Duplicate acknowledgements are generated by later segments arriving out of order. If nothing follows the lost segment, none are generated, so fast retransmit cannot fire and the loss is detected by the retransmission timer — with cwnd restarted at one segment rather than halved to the threshold.

This is the counterexample to "TCP always detects loss by duplicate ACKs". It also explains why the number of dup ACKs a sender sees depends on how much data follows the hole.

The threshold floor of two segments

Condition: the window at the loss is very small — one or two segments.

Halving one segment gives zero, which would forbid transmission. The rule therefore floors the threshold at two segments: $$\text{ssthresh} = \max(\text{flight}/2,\ 2\ \text{SMSS}).$$

A trace that halves 1 MSS to 0 has applied the formula without its guard.

When the receiver window binds first

Condition: rwnd is smaller than cwnd over the whole range the question covers.

The congestion window may keep growing in the sender's own bookkeeping, but the bytes on the wire stop at rwnd. In the GATE 2020 trace the answer stayed below 50 KB, so rwnd never bound; if the trace had reached 25 MSS the answer would have been capped there and the question would have had a different shape.

Always check the final value against rwnd. A trace that crosses it has not finished the question.

Nagle plus delayed ACK

Condition: an application writes small records, the sender has Nagle enabled, and the receiver delays acknowledgements.

The sender holds the second small segment until the first is acknowledged; the receiver waits for a second full segment or about half a second before acknowledging. Each waits for the other, so the transfer crawls at roughly one record per round trip instead of one per window.

The fix is at the application or at the socket option, not in the congestion algorithm.

An MSS larger than the path allows

Condition: the sender's MSS was chosen from its own link MTU, but a later link is smaller.

The segment is then fragmented at the IP layer, and the loss of any fragment costs the whole segment a retransmission. Path MTU discovery exists to avoid exactly this, and it is why the fragmentation lesson's arithmetic reaches into this one.

MSS is not a constant of TCP. It is a function of the smallest MTU on the path.

Three exam conventions that are not in the RFC

Initial window. RFC 5681 allows up to four segments; almost every GATE trace starts at one segment, and the question says so when it differs.

Which variant. "Three duplicate ACKs" behaves as Tahoe (restart at one) or Reno (restart at the threshold) depending on the question. If neither is named, say which you assumed.

Counting the loss round trip. A published key that accepts a range is telling you the convention is arguable. Write your convention next to your answer.

Six beliefs that produce wrong answers, and the smallest fact that refutes each

A misconception is not a slip of arithmetic; it is a rule that is wrong and will keep producing wrong answers. Each card gives the belief, why it tempts, the minimal counterexample, and the correct rule.

"In slow start, cwnd doubles on every acknowledgement."

Why it tempts. Slow start really does double per round trip, and an acknowledgement is what arrives at the sender, so the two get merged.

Counterexample. Start at 1 MSS. If cwnd doubled per ACK, two ACKs would give 4 MSS within one round trip. The actual sequence is 1 → 2 after the first window of ACKs, because each ACK adds one segment and there are only as many ACKs as segments in the window.

Correct rule. +1 MSS per ACK; a doubling per round trip. GATE 2018 Q14 turns on exactly this distinction.

"Slow start is the slow phase."

Why it tempts. The name.

Counterexample. From 1 MSS, slow start reaches 16 MSS in four round trips; congestion avoidance needs eight more round trips to go from 8 to 16. Slow start is the fast phase.

Correct rule. "Slow" describes the starting point, not the rate. Exponential versus linear is the distinction to hold on to.

"A timeout and three duplicate ACKs do the same thing."

Why it tempts. Both halve the threshold, so the first line of the trace is identical.

Counterexample. GATE 2024 Set 2 Q54: from 12 MSS with two clean round trips, a timeout gives 4 MSS and Reno's duplicate ACKs give 8 MSS.

Correct rule. Both halve the threshold; only the timeout resets the window to one segment. The duplicate ACKs say the path is still delivering, which is why the cut is smaller.

"The slow-start threshold starts at half the receiver window."

Why it tempts. The threshold is half of something, and the receiver window is the only window printed in the question.

Counterexample. GATE 2020 Q55 states a threshold of 32 KB with a receiver window of 50 KB. Half of 50 is 25, which is not the threshold and gives a different answer.

Correct rule. The threshold is halved from the data in flight at a loss. If no loss has occurred and the question gives no threshold, the question is incomplete — do not invent one.

"cwnd can never exceed rwnd."

Why it tempts. The cap rule is stated as a minimum, so the two numbers look coupled.

Counterexample. The sender's cwnd can grow past rwnd in its own bookkeeping — nothing on the wire reveals cwnd, so nothing stops it. The bytes in flight are what stop at rwnd.

Correct rule. The minimum applies to data in flight, not to the variable. A question about "the congestion window" and a question about "the data sent" can therefore have different answers.

"The advertised window is a number of segments."

Why it tempts. Every trace in this clause is written in segments, so the units blur.

Counterexample. A window field of 50 KB with a 2 KB segment is 25 segments; treating 50 as a segment count gives a window 25 times too large, and the 2020 trace then never reaches it.

Correct rule. The field counts bytes. Convert to segments at the top of your working, and write the unit beside every number.

A belief that is only half wrong: "flow control and congestion control are the same mechanism"

They share the shape — a window that the sender respects — which is why the sentence is tempting. They differ in what the window protects, who sets it, and how it is learned: rwnd protects the receiver buffer and is published in every ACK; cwnd protects the network and is inferred from loss.

The separating test: on a path with no loss at all, does the rate still have a ceiling? If yes, that ceiling is flow control, because congestion control has nothing to react to. The animation's second scene computes that ceiling: 65535 bytes over a 100 ms round trip is about 5.24 Mbps however fast the link is.

Six question shapes, and the reasoning each one demands

These are patterns observed in past papers of this clause, described as patterns and not as predictions. Each card names the shape, the procedure, and the wrong approach that the distractors are built from.

Pattern 1 — the window at a given time

Shape. A round trip, a segment size, a threshold, sometimes a receiver window, and a time or a number of round trips. Find cwnd.

Procedure. Divide the elapsed time by the round trip. Convert everything to segments. Walk the trace: double below the threshold, +1 at or above it, cap at rwnd.

The distractor. One option always continues doubling past the threshold; another always halves rwnd to invent a threshold. Seen in GATE 2020 Q55 and GATE 2024 Set 2 Q54.

Pattern 2 — the time to reach a target window

Shape. The window at a loss, the round trip, and a target. Find the time, or the number of round trips.

Procedure. Halve to get the threshold, restart at one, count the doublings to the threshold, then count the linear steps to the target, then multiply by the round trip.

The distractor. Pure doubling gives a much smaller answer, and forgetting the endpoint gives one round trip too few. Seen in GATE 2014 Set 1 Q27, where the key accepts a range because of the counting convention.

Pattern 3 — which statement about growth is true

Shape. Four statements about per-ACK and per-round-trip growth; pick the true one, or the set of true ones.

Procedure. Check each statement against the phase it claims. "+2 MSS per ACK" is wrong in both phases; "doubles per ACK" is wrong; "one MSS per round trip" is congestion avoidance; "doubles per round trip" is slow start.

The distractor. The statement that is true of the other phase. Seen in GATE 2018 Q14.

Pattern 4 — throughput, or the window a path needs

Shape. Bandwidth and round trip, sometimes a peak window and a segment size. Find an average rate, or the window that fills the pipe.

Procedure. Average window = 0.75 × peak; divide by the round trip; convert bytes to bits only at the end. For the other direction, BDP = bandwidth × round trip, then divide by MSS for segments.

The distractor. Using the peak instead of the average, or the BDP in bits compared against a window in bytes.

Pattern 5 — the socket API

Shape. The correct order of calls, or which call makes a socket passive, or what accept returns.

Procedure. Server: socket, bind, listen, accept, recv. Client: socket, connect. listen is the passive call; accept returns a new socket; connect is where the SYN goes.

The distractor. accept offered before listen. Seen in GATE 2015 Set 2 Q20 and GATE 2014 Set 2 Q24.

Pattern 6 — the handshake and the header

Shape. Which flags are set in each of the three handshake packets, which sequence and acknowledgement numbers they carry, or how many bytes of a segment are header.

Procedure. SYN carries SYN only; the second packet carries SYN and ACK; the third carries ACK only. The acknowledgement number is the peer's sequence number plus one. Header arithmetic: 20 + 20 bytes off the MTU.

Recent shape. A 2025 Set 1 multiple-select item asks which flags are set in each of the three packets, and a 2024 Set 1 item asks about the sequence and acknowledgement numbers in SYN and SYN-ACK.

The decision procedure, before any arithmetic

Step 1. Which window binds over the range asked — rwnd or cwnd? That decides whether the receiver's buffer can affect the answer at all.

Step 2. Which signal fired — a timeout, three duplicate ACKs, or neither? That decides the window immediately after a loss.

Step 3. Which variant — Tahoe, or Reno? Only matters for duplicate ACKs, and only if the question does not state the rule.

Step 4. Convert to segments, walk the trace, and stop at the round trip named. Write the unit beside every number.

NATOriginal practice

A TCP sender's congestion window is 24 MSS when a retransmission timeout occurs. Assume the window is restarted at one segment and grows by the standard rules with no further loss. The congestion window, in MSS, after two further round trips is ______.

Answer: 4

ssthresh = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 and cwnd = 1. Round trip one: 1 < 12, so doubling gives 2. Round trip two: 2 < 12, so doubling gives 4.

Why 6 and 8 are wrong. Both come from applying the threshold as if it were 6, i.e. halving twice. Why 2 is wrong. It counts one round trip instead of two.

MCQGATE CSE 2018 Q14

Consider the following statements regarding the slow start phase of the TCP congestion control algorithm, where cwnd is the congestion window and MSS the maximum segment size.

(i) cwnd increases by 2 MSS on every successful acknowledgement.
(ii) cwnd approximately doubles on every successful acknowledgement.
(iii) cwnd increases by 1 MSS every round trip time.
(iv) cwnd approximately doubles every round trip time.

Which one of the following is correct?

  1. Only (ii) and (iii) are true
  2. Only (i) and (iii) are true
  3. Only (iv) is true
  4. Only (i) and (iv) are true

Answer: C — only (iv) is true

Per acknowledgement cwnd grows by one segment, so a whole window of acknowledgements doubles it. (i) and (ii) are false; (iii) describes congestion avoidance.

Why D tempts. It pairs the one true statement with the one that sounds most like it, "doubling per ACK". The rate per ACK is additive; the rate per round trip is multiplicative.

NATGATE CSE 2020 Q55

A TCP connection has round trip time 6 ms, receiver advertised window 50 KB, slow-start threshold 32 KB and MSS 2 KB. The connection is established at t = 0 with no timeouts and no errors. The size of the congestion window, in KB, at t + 60 ms after all acknowledgements are processed is ______.

Answer: 44

60 ÷ 6 = 10 round trips. Doubling gives 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 KB after four round trips; then +2 KB per round trip gives 34, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44 KB over six more round trips. The receiver window of 50 KB never binds.

Why 42 tempts. It stops one round trip early. Why 25 appears in discussions. Halving the receiver window to invent a threshold, which the question does not ask for.

NATGATE CSE 2014 Set 1 Q27

The size of the congestion window of a TCP connection is 32 KB when a timeout occurs. The round trip time is 100 ms and the maximum segment size is 2 KB. The time taken, in ms, for the connection to get back to a 32 KB congestion window is ______.

Answer: 1100 (the published key accepts a range around 1000–1300)

16 MSS at the timeout, so ssthresh = 8 and cwnd = 1. Doubling gives 1, 2, 4, 8; linear gives 9 … 16, reached at round trip 11. Eleven round trips of 100 ms is 1100 ms.

Why 400 tempts. Doubling from 1 to 16 ignores the threshold. Why the range exists. Some solutions count the round trip in which the timeout occurs; state your convention.

NATGATE CSE 2024 Set 2 Q54

A TCP connection is operating with a congestion window of 12 MSS when a timeout occurs due to packet loss. Assuming all segments transmitted in the next two round trips are acknowledged correctly, the congestion window size, in MSS, is ______.

Answer: 4

ssthresh = 6, cwnd = 1. Round trip one gives 2, round trip two gives 4, both still below the threshold.

Why 6 is wrong. It jumps straight to the threshold. Why 8 is wrong. It doubles past the threshold — and it is the right answer only if the loss were signalled by three duplicate ACKs under Reno.

MCQGATE CSE 2014 Set 2 Q24

Which one of the following socket API functions converts an unconnected active TCP socket into a passive socket?

  1. connect
  2. bind
  3. listen
  4. accept

Answer: C — listen

listen marks the socket as willing to accept incoming connections, which is exactly the passive-open state. bind only attaches a local address and port.

Why accept is wrong. accept does not change the listening socket at all; it returns a new socket for one connection, while the original stays passive.

MCQGATE CSE 2015 Set 2 Q20

Identify the correct order in which a server process must invoke the function calls accept, bind, listen and recv according to the UNIX socket API.

  1. listen, accept, bind, recv
  2. bind, listen, accept, recv
  3. bind, accept, listen, recv
  4. accept, listen, bind, recv

Answer: B — bind, listen, accept, recv

A socket must be named before it can be made passive, and made passive before a connection can be taken from it. Only then can data be read.

Why A and D fail. Both call accept before the socket is listening, so there is no connection queue to take from. Why C fails. accept on a socket that never listened fails with an invalid-argument error.

MSQOriginal practice

Which of the following statements about flow control and congestion control in TCP is/are TRUE?

  1. Flow control prevents a fast sender from overrunning the receiver's buffer.
  2. The congestion window is communicated to the sender in a field of the TCP header.
  3. The data a sender may have in flight is bounded by the smaller of the two windows.
  4. If the receiver advertises a larger window, the sender can always send more data immediately.

Answer: A and C

A is the definition of flow control. C is the cap rule. B is false: no header field carries cwnd; it is inferred from loss. D is false: cwnd may be the smaller window, in which case a larger rwnd changes nothing.

Trap. D is the sentence that separates the two ideas, and it is the option most often marked true by mistake.

MCQOriginal practice

A sender's congestion window is 4 MSS. The last segment of that window is lost, and the sender has no further data to send. How is the loss detected?

  1. By three duplicate acknowledgements, so fast retransmit fires
  2. By the retransmission timeout, because no duplicate acknowledgements are generated
  3. By a negative acknowledgement from the receiver
  4. By the receiver advertising a zero window

Answer: B

Duplicate acknowledgements are produced by later segments arriving out of order. With nothing after the lost segment, none are produced, so the timer is the only detector — and the window restarts at one segment rather than being cut to the threshold.

Why A tempts. It applies "TCP detects loss by duplicate ACKs" without its condition. Why C is wrong. TCP has no NACK; the receiver only repeats its cumulative acknowledgement.

NATOriginal practice

A TCP connection under additive increase and multiplicative decrease has a peak congestion window of 80 MSS, an MSS of 1000 bytes and a round trip time of 50 ms. Its average throughput, in Mbps, is ______.

Answer: 9.6

Average window = 0.75 × 80 = 60 MSS = 60000 bytes per round trip. 60000 ÷ 0.05 = 1200000 bytes per second, which is 9.6 Mbps.

Why 12.8 tempts. It uses the peak window instead of the average. Why 1.2 tempts. It forgets the conversion from bytes to bits.

The two windows

  • In flight ≤ min(rwnd, cwnd). The smaller binds.
  • rwnd: 16-bit header field, in bytes, from the acknowledgement number. Flow control.
  • cwnd: sender-side only, never transmitted. Congestion control.
  • Usable = rwnd − (last byte sent − last byte acknowledged).
  • Scaling multiplies the advertised field by a power of two agreed at setup.

Growth rules

  • Slow start, below ssthresh: +1 MSS per ACK, so a doubling per round trip.
  • Congestion avoidance, at or above ssthresh: +1 MSS per round trip.
  • Doubling never lands past the threshold.
  • Timeout: ssthresh = max(flight/2, 2 MSS), cwnd = 1.
  • Three duplicate ACKs: ssthresh = flight/2; Tahoe sets cwnd = 1, Reno sets cwnd = ssthresh.

Numbers to have ready

  • 2014 Set 1: 16 → threshold 8 → 1, 2, 4, 8, 9 … 16 → 11 RTT → 1100 ms.
  • 2020: 10 RTT → 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 KB after four → +2 KB for six more → 44 KB.
  • 2024 Set 2: 12 → threshold 6 → timeout gives 4, duplicate ACKs give 8.
  • Average rate = 0.75 × W_max ÷ RTT; 100 MSS × 1460 B ÷ 40 ms = 21.9 Mbps.
  • BDP = bandwidth × RTT; 100 Mbps × 100 ms = 1.25 MB.

Sockets and segments

  • Server: socket, bind, listen, accept, recv/send, close.
  • Client: socket, connect, send/recv, close.
  • listen = passive open; accept returns a new connected socket.
  • Handshake: SYN, SYN + ACK, ACK. connect sends the SYN.
  • MSS = MTU − 40 for IPv4 TCP, − 60 for IPv6. 1500 → 1460.
  • Connection identity = (source IP, source port, destination IP, destination port).
  • Only the active closer waits in TIME_WAIT, for 2 × MSL.

Decision procedure in one line each

Window at a time. Time ÷ RTT → convert to segments → double below the threshold → +1 at it → cap at rwnd.

Time to a target. Halve to the threshold → count doublings → count linear steps → multiply by the RTT.

Throughput. 0.75 × peak ÷ RTT, then bytes to bits at the last step.

Socket question. Name the side first (server or client), then recite the order; the distractors are always a permutation.

The four slips, one last time

Doubling past the threshold · halving the receiver window to invent a threshold · reading per-ACK growth as per-RTT growth · treating flow control and congestion control as one limit. Write the unit and the loss signal at the top of your working and none of the four survives.

Official and standards

  • GATE 2027 CS syllabus, IIT Madras, CS_GATE2027_Syllabus.pdf, read on 28 October 2026. Supports the Section 10 wording quoted in Overview, including that flow control, congestion control and the socket API are named in one clause.
  • RFC 5681 (TCP Congestion Control, 2009). The four algorithms — slow start, congestion avoidance, fast retransmit, fast recovery — the per-ACK growth rules, the halving of the threshold and the three-duplicate-ACK trigger. Read for this lesson: the abstract, the table of contents and the definitions of section 2; the growth rules are stated here in the form that section gives them.
