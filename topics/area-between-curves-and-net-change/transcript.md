## Scene 1. Signed area is not geometric area

*0:00 – 0:30 · objective: Separate the two questions a definite integral can be asked: the signed total, where pieces below the axis count negative, and the geometric area of the same region, where they do not. Settle it on one function that crosses the axis twice.*

**0:00–0:07** — A strip above the axis counts positive, a strip below it counts negative.

> Caption: An integral counts area below the axis as negative.

**0:07–0:15** — The curve crosses at 0 and 2, and spends the interval between them below the axis.

> Caption: x squared minus 2x is below the axis between 0 and 2, above it after 2.

**0:15–0:22** — Minus four thirds and plus four thirds cancel, so the signed total is zero.

> Caption: The two pieces cancel: minus 4/3 plus 4/3 is 0.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *A zero answer does not mean the region has no area. It means the signed contributions balanced. Read what the question asked for before concluding anything from the zero.*

**0:22–0:30** — Absolute value each piece and the area is eight thirds. Two questions, two answers.

> Caption: The geometric area of the same region is 8/3, about 2.666667.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *The words that decide which question is being asked are "the integral of" against "the area enclosed". They are not interchangeable.*

## Scene 2. Area between two curves is one difference

*0:30 – 1:00 · objective: Show that the area between an upper and a lower curve is the integral of upper minus lower, that the crossings decide where to stop and start, and that the same rule covers a region whose curves swap order.*

**0:30–0:37** — The strip height is upper minus lower, so the area is the integral of that difference.

> Caption: Between two curves, integrate the upper one minus the lower one.

**0:37–0:45** — x above x squared on that interval gives one sixth.

> Caption: On 0 to 1, y equals x sits above y equals x squared.

**0:45–0:52** — Set the curves equal, factor, and the crossings become the limits.

> Caption: Find the crossings first: they are the limits.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *The limits are not given by the question wording; they are computed. Taking 0 to 3 because 3 appears in the algebra drops the part of the region left of the axis.*

**0:52–1:00** — Thirty-two thirds, about ten point six seven.

> Caption: The area is 32/3, about 10.666667.

## Scene 3. Choose the variable, then check the answer did not change

*1:00 – 1:30 · objective: Slice the same region vertically and horizontally, see that the second slicing needs a curve read backwards as x of y, and use the agreement of the two answers as a check rather than as an assumption.*

**1:00–1:07** — Vertical strips or horizontal strips: same region, same area.

> Caption: The same region can be sliced two ways.

**1:07–1:15** — Read the curves backwards: x as a function of y.

> Caption: A horizontal slice needs a curve read backwards, as x of y.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *The inverse of y equals x squared on the whole line does not exist, because the function is not one to one. On this region only the right branch is used, which is why the square root appears without a plus or minus.*

**1:15–1:22** — Vertical: one third.

> Caption: Vertical slicing gives 1/3.

**1:22–1:30** — Horizontal: one third as well. The agreement is the check.

> Caption: Horizontal slicing gives 1/3 as well.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Agreement proves the two integrals describe the same region. It does not prove the region was the one the question asked for, so read the bounding curves again.*

## Scene 4. The accumulation function and its derivative

*1:30 – 2:00 · objective: Treat the integral with a moving upper limit as a function in its own right, see its difference quotient fall onto the integrand as the step shrinks, and understand why a constant added to it changes the level but not the rate.*

**1:30–1:37** — Let the top limit move and the integral becomes a function of where you stopped.

> Caption: With a moving upper limit the integral becomes a function.

**1:37–1:45** — Shrink the step and the quotient lands on the height of the curve.

> Caption: Its difference quotient falls onto the integrand.

**1:45–1:52** — The rate at which area accumulates is the height of the curve.

> Caption: The derivative of the accumulated area is the current height.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *The statement needs the integrand continuous at that point. Where the integrand jumps, the accumulation function is still continuous but has a corner, and a question can ask exactly that.*

**1:52–2:00** — A constant shifts the level. It cannot change a rate, so it cancels in a definite integral.

> Caption: Adding a constant changes the level, never the rate.

## Scene 5. Net change: integrating a rate

*2:00 – 2:30 · objective: Read the integral of a rate as the net change in the quantity, and separate it from the total amount travelled when the rate changes sign. One velocity curve, two different answers, both correct for their question.*

**2:00–2:07** — The integral of a rate is the net change in the quantity.

> Caption: Integrating a rate gives the net change, not the total activity.

**2:07–2:15** — Positive, then negative, then positive again: forward, backward, forward.

> Caption: This velocity is negative in the middle of the interval.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *A velocity that changes sign does not mean the object stopped permanently at those points. It means the direction reversed at that instant, and the position is still continuous.*

**2:15–2:22** — The signed total is four thirds. That is the displacement.

> Caption: The three pieces are 4/3, minus 4/3 and 4/3, so the displacement is 4/3.

**2:22–2:30** — Distance counts every piece positively and comes to four.

> Caption: Distance travelled takes absolute values, and comes to 4.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *The two answers differ by a factor of three here, which is why both appear in an option list. The deciding word is displacement against distance, or net change against total.*

## Scene 6. Average value and where it is attained

**2:30–2:37** — Average value is the integral divided by the width of the interval.

> Caption: The average value of a function is its integral over the width.

**2:37–2:45** — A rectangle of that height has the same area as the region.

> Caption: It is the height of the rectangle with the same area.

**2:45–2:52** — Two over pi, about zero point six three six six two.

> Caption: For sine over 0 to pi the average is 2 over pi, about 0.636620.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Averaging the two endpoint values is the commonest wrong method, and here it gives zero. The trapezoid rule averages endpoints to estimate an integral; that is a different quantity from the average value of the function.*

**2:52–3:00** — Two points, not one. The theorem promises existence, not uniqueness.

> Caption: Two points attain it: 0.690107 and 2.451486.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Solving for the point and finding two answers is not an error. Stopping after the first is, if the question asked for all of them.*

## Scene 7. Probability is an area

**3:00–3:07** — Probability between two points is the area under the density between them.

> Caption: A probability is an area under a density.

**3:07–3:15** — It is F of b minus F of a, the accumulation function read twice.

> Caption: That area is a difference of two values of the distribution function.

**3:15–3:22** — Zero point three eight three four zero, by two independent routes.

> Caption: Exponential with rate 0.5: the probability between 1 and 3 is 0.383400.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Subtracting the densities rather than the distribution functions gives the wrong number, because a density is not a probability. Only its integral is.*

**3:22–3:30** — Zero point six eight two six eight nine for the normal, exactly one half for the uniform.

> Caption: Normal between minus 1 and 1 is 0.682689; uniform between 1 and 3 is 0.5.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *A uniform density whose height looks small can still carry a large probability, because the probability is height times width. Reading the height as the probability is the error.*

## Scene 8. Areas under rate graphs elsewhere in the syllabus

*3:30 – 4:00 · objective: Carry the same three readings — total, average, and probability — into a rate graph that is not a physics curve, and see that total is the area, average is the area over the width, and a utilisation is the average of an indicator.*

**3:30–3:37** — Total is the area, average is the area over the width, a fraction is an average of ones and zeros.

> Caption: The same three readings work on any rate graph.

**3:37–3:45** — Four pi, because the sine contributes nothing over a full period.

> Caption: Over 0 to 2 pi the total is 4 pi, about 12.566371.

**3:45–3:52** — Four pi over two pi is two. The ripple averages away.

> Caption: The average rate is exactly 2.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *Averaging over half a period does not remove the sine term. The cancellation needs a whole number of periods, and the interval is part of the question.*

**3:52–4:00** — Utilisation is the average of an indicator, which is a fraction of time.

> Caption: An average of ones and zeros is a fraction of time.

Exam-trap caption mode adds: *This is an educational reading of the arithmetic, not a syllabus formula. The syllabus names performance metrics without prescribing this derivation, so treat it as a way to remember the arithmetic.*
