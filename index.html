Distance Vector and Link State Routing

Two families, one question: what may a router know? Distance vector is the Bellman-Ford equation run locally - add your link cost to each neighbour's claim and keep the minimum - so the cost and the next hop fall out of the same computation. Its disease is provenance: nothing records where a number came from, so a dead route climbs 2, 3, 4 ... 16 and only RIP's ceiling of 16 stops it, which is also why a RIP path is at most 15 hops. Link state floods each router's own links until every router holds the same map, then runs Dijkstra from itself, which is why its trees differ while its databases do not. TCP Flow Control, Congestion Control and the Socket API

Two windows and two signals. A sender may have in flight at most min(rwnd, cwnd), and the smaller one names the limit: rwnd is a 16-bit byte count in every ACK (at most 65535, scaled by an option), cwnd is inferred from loss and never transmitted. Flow control is a budget - usable = rwnd minus bytes in flight, so 6 MSS advertised with 2 unacknowledged leaves 4, and on a lossless 100 ms path the 16-bit ceiling alone caps the rate at 5.24 Mbps. Congestion control is a curve: slow start adds one MSS per ACK and therefore doubles per round trip until ssthresh, then congestion avoidance adds one per round trip; a timeout sets ssthresh to half the flight and cwnd to 1, three duplicate ACKs set cwnd to the threshold under Reno. Calculus: Limits, Extrema and Integration

Four decisions, one habit. A limit is about a neighbourhood, so substituting is the first move and 0/0 is a signal rather than an answer: root(2x+22)-4 over x+3 at -3 is 0/0, the derivative quotient is 1/root(2x+22), and the value is 0.25 (GATE 2021 Set 1), with seven sample points either side agreeing. Continuity is one equation with three requirements, and a divergent term cannot be matched to a finite value: c1 e^x - c2 ln(1/x) at 3 forces c2 = 0 and c1 = 3, so the sum is 3 (2026 Set 1). Matrices, Systems and Eigenvalues

Four quantities and the questions they answer. The determinant is the area factor: 5 for rows (2,1),(1,3) and -2 for (1,2),(3,4), a swap negates it, and it multiplies over products, so det(2I) is 4 while 1 + 1 is 2. Rank is the count of pivots: the rows 1..9 matrix has rank 2 and nullity 1, and rank plus nullity is the number of columns, never the rows. Two ranks decide a system: equal means consistent, a larger augmented rank means none, and columns minus rank is the count of free variables, so a 3x5 matrix of rank 2 has 3 of them. A wide n by m with m > n has nullity at least m - n, which is why GATE 2024 Set 1 Q39's 'at least' is the only true option. One question asked five ways. A probability is a count of equally likely outcomes and then a measure obeying three axioms; conditioning shrinks the space from 36 dice cells to 6, so P(sum = 11 | sum > 9) is 2/6 = 1/3 rather than 2/36.
